How to read this chart

Start on the left with your machine family. Then verify the SV code on the body plate, the voltage and capacity printed on the old pack, and the way the pack attaches. SV codes below are examples reported in the market, not a guarantee that every production batch uses the same pack.

Why two Dyson packs that look alike may not fit

  1. Voltage class. Older 21.6 V and 22.2 V platforms are not interchangeable with 25.2 V-class packs.
  2. Connector and pinout. A plug that nearly lines up can leave contacts unconnected.
  3. Battery management. Modern packs communicate charge and temperature; a mismatched pack may trigger a fault or refuse to charge.
  4. Shell and mounting. Screws, catches and balance change between generations.
Safety firstNever open, splice, short or adapt a lithium-ion pack. Stop using a swollen, hot, leaking or damaged battery and recycle it through an approved program.

Frequently asked questions

Can I put a V8 battery in a V6?

Usually no. V6 and V8 belong to different pack families even where sellers quote the same voltage class. Match the part number and connector.

Do V11 and V15 use the same battery?

Some click-in variants share a platform, but the only reliable answer is the part number on your original pack. Do not assume from the model name.

What if my SV code is not listed?

Send the SV code, the battery part number, voltage and a photo of the battery bay. An unlisted code is a reason to check, not to guess.
