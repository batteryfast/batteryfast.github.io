Dyson DC handheld battery guide

Older Dyson handhelds span several battery platforms. The DC number is a useful start, but the chemistry, voltage and connector printed on the pack decide compatibility.

A note on wording: Batteryfast is an independent compatibility guidance publisher, not the original equipment manufacturer. Brand and model names below identify the machine platform only.

Reviewed 2026-11-25 by the Batteryfast compatibility desk · Specifications must be confirmed on your own battery label.

Check the label before you orderTreat the voltage, capacity (Ah/Wh), part number and connector printed on your original pack as the source of truth. A similar-looking shell is not proof of fit.

How to identify an old Dyson handheld pack

  1. Find the DC number on the machine plate.
  2. Remove the battery and record voltage, capacity and chemistry.
  3. Note whether the pack slides, clicks or screws into the handle.
  4. Compare the part number, not just the photo, with a listing.

When replacement is not worthwhile

For very old handhelds, compare the cost of a quality pack with the condition of the machine, charger and floor tools. A new battery will not fix a worn motor or a damaged charger.

Frequently asked questions

Can a DC58 pack fit a DC31?

Do not assume so. DC58 and DC31 are commonly listed on different voltage platforms and with different mounting styles.

Do I need the original Dyson charger?

Use a charger specified for your battery platform. If the charger is damaged, replace it with a correct, certified unit.

Are old Ni-MH packs still available?

Some suppliers list them, but confirm chemistry and capacity. Do not substitute lithium-ion without an explicit compatibility confirmation.
