Voltage, mAh and watt-hours explained

Two packs with the same mAh can store very different amounts of energy if their voltages differ. That is why watt-hours are the fairest comparison.

A note on wording: Batteryfast is an independent compatibility guidance publisher, not the original equipment manufacturer. Brand and model names below identify the machine platform only.

Reviewed 2026-11-25 by the Batteryfast compatibility desk · Specifications must be confirmed on your own battery label.

Check the label before you orderTreat the voltage, capacity (Ah/Wh), part number and connector printed on your original pack as the source of truth. A similar-looking shell is not proof of fit.

The three numbers on a label

Voltage (V)
Electrical pressure of the pack
Capacity (Ah or mAh)
Charge stored; 4000 mAh = 4.0 Ah
Energy (Wh)
V x Ah; the useful comparison figure
Chemistry
Li-ion, NiMH and others behave differently

Worked examples

21.6 V x 4.0 Ah = 86.4 Wh. 25.2 V x 4.0 Ah = 100.8 Wh. The second pack stores about 17 percent more energy at the same mAh figure.

Same machine onlyCompare watt-hours only among packs confirmed to fit your machine. Voltage and connector must match the original.

Why advertised runtime differs from real use

  • Bench figures often use low-power mode without a motorised head.
  • Boost mode and heavy debris increase current draw.
  • Ageing cells hold less charge and sag under load.
  • For robots, sensors, brushes and bin condition also affect docking behaviour.

Frequently asked questions

Is a higher voltage always better?

No. The machine is designed for a voltage platform. Higher voltage in the wrong pack is unsafe.

Why do some sellers list only mAh?

It is a marketing shortcut. Ask for the voltage and watt-hour figure.

Does capacity affect charging time?

It can, but charger design and battery management also matter.
