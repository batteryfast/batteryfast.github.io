Practical replacement vacuum battery guide

This is the short version of the whole method: identify, verify, compare, buy, install and recycle. Follow it in order and most costly mistakes disappear.

A note on wording: Batteryfast is an independent compatibility guidance publisher, not the original equipment manufacturer. Brand and model names below identify the machine platform only.

Reviewed 2026-11-25 by the Batteryfast compatibility desk · Specifications must be confirmed on your own battery label.

Check the label before you orderTreat the voltage, capacity (Ah/Wh), part number and connector printed on your original pack as the source of truth. A similar-looking shell is not proof of fit.

1. Identify the exact machine

Record the full model name including suffixes. For Dyson, add the SV code from the plate. For Roomba, add the model sticker underneath and whether the robot is a Combo.

2. Verify the pack

Read the part number, voltage, capacity and chemistry on the old battery. Compare with the relevant chart. The five-field check explains each field in detail.

Read the Batteryfast battery compatibility guide or a broader guide from Batteries Company.

3. Compare like with like

Use watt-hours, warranty terms and written fit confirmation. Do not compare a verified pack against a listing with unverifiable capacity. See runtime versus price and genuine versus aftermarket.

4. Buy, install and recycle

Choose a seller with clear regional terms. Fit the pack without force, charge fully and keep proof of purchase. Then recycle the old pack through an approved route: AU, UK and US recycling guide.

Next step

When you are satisfied that the voltage, capacity, code and connector match your original pack, send the details to the Batteryfast fit check desk. The battery guide, warranty and returns policy and recycling notes are one click away.

Frequently asked questions

What is the single most important check?

The part number on your original pack. Model names and photos are less reliable.

How do I avoid inflated capacity claims?

Ask for voltage and watt-hours, not just mAh, and buy from a seller with clear warranty terms.
