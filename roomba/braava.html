Braava jet M6 battery guide

Braava robots mop rather than vacuum, and their packs are not part of the Roomba vacuum battery families. This page keeps those two worlds separate.

A note on wording: Batteryfast is an independent compatibility guidance publisher, not the original equipment manufacturer. Brand and model names below identify the machine platform only.

Reviewed 2026-11-25 by the Batteryfast compatibility desk · Specifications must be confirmed on your own battery label.

Check the label before you orderTreat the voltage, capacity (Ah/Wh), part number and connector printed on your original pack as the source of truth. A similar-looking shell is not proof of fit.

Reported capacity
Around 19 Wh in many listings
Do not cross-fitA Braava mop pack is not a Roomba vacuum pack. Do not substitute ABL-D1, ABL-D2, ABL-B or 4376392.

Why mop packs are different

Mopping robots have different power profiles, chassis shapes and docking behaviour. Even a pack with a similar voltage cannot be assumed to fit or communicate correctly.

Safety firstNever open, splice, short or adapt a lithium-ion pack. Stop using a swollen, hot, leaking or damaged battery and recycle it through an approved program.

Older Braava models

Older Braava 300-series robots are commonly associated with NiMH packs and different part numbers. If you own one, match the chemistry and part number on the original battery rather than using the M6 guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Is ABL-C the same as ABL-D1?

No. ABL-C is listed for Braava mops; ABL-D1 is listed for Roomba vacuum robots. They are different families.

How do I find my Braava model number?

Flip the robot and read the sticker. The M6 variants are commonly listed with M611020 and related numbers.

Can I recycle an old Braava pack with other batteries?

Yes, through an approved rechargeable battery collection program. Protect the terminals first.
