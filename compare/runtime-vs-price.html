Battery runtime versus price

The cheapest pack per mAh is not always the cheapest pack per useful hour. Use a consistent measure and only compare batteries that fit the same machine.

A note on wording: Batteryfast is an independent compatibility guidance publisher, not the original equipment manufacturer. Brand and model names below identify the machine platform only.

Reviewed 2026-11-25 by the Batteryfast compatibility desk · Specifications must be confirmed on your own battery label.

Check the label before you orderTreat the voltage, capacity (Ah/Wh), part number and connector printed on your original pack as the source of truth. A similar-looking shell is not proof of fit.

A simple value framework

Step 1
Confirm fit and part number
Step 2
Convert V x Ah to Wh
Step 3
Divide delivered price by Wh
Step 4
Adjust for warranty and return terms
Step 5
Reject unverified capacity claims

Example: 21.6 V x 4.0 Ah = 86.4 Wh. A 25.2 V x 4.0 Ah pack = 100.8 Wh. Same mAh, different stored energy.

Why runtime claims differ

  • Low-power test figures are not real-world cleaning figures.
  • Motorised heads, boost mode and filters change consumption.
  • Cell ageing reduces usable capacity over time.
  • A robot returning to dock early may have sensor or bin issues, not just a battery issue.

Frequently asked questions

Is higher mAh always worth paying for?

Only within the same voltage platform and with an honest rating. Compare watt-hours and warranty.

How much runtime will a new pack add?

It depends on the old pack's condition and your cleaning mode. Avoid fixed promises from any seller.

Should I compare prices across different models?

No. First restrict the comparison to packs that fit your machine.
